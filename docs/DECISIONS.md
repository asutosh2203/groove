# Decisions

Design principles and the decisions register for Dynamic Island for KDE Plasma. Newest entries at
the bottom. Status is **Accepted** unless noted. Superseded entries stay, marked as such.

This project is a fork of [Manu028's kde-plasma-dynamic-island](https://github.com/manu028-727/kde-plasma-dynamic-island)
(GPL-3.0-or-later), inspired by [TaqsBlaze/notched](https://github.com/TaqsBlaze/notched),
DynamicNotch and NexNotch.

## Design principles
1. **One shell, two appearances.** Dynamic Notch and Dynamic Pill are presentation modes of the same
   product, not separate widgets with duplicated logic.
2. **Fixed anchor, independent expansion.** The panel supplies a stable compact shell; expanded
   content renders in a separate Plasma dialog instead of resizing the panel.
3. **Activity-first behaviour.** Media and notifications temporarily replace idle content.
   Notifications pre-empt media; media returns afterwards.
4. **Preserve proven interactions.** Click, double-click, click-outside dismissal, media switching and
   notification lifecycle stay stable unless a change is agreed.
5. **Native KDE/Qt infrastructure first.** Prefer Plasma and Qt services (D-Bus, QML modules) over
   shelling out or native helpers.
6. **Polish through composition, not decoration.** Shared geometry, spacing and alignment over ornament.
7. **Development-only iteration.** Validate in a development install; production stays untouched until
   a change is accepted.
8. **Collaborative decisions.** Implementation is driven by investigation; design and scope decisions are
   made through review of findings, screenshots and trade-offs.
9. **Lightweight by default.** When nothing changes, nothing renders. No background subprocesses while
   idle. Every timer and animation has a stop condition. Beauty never at the cost of stutter.
10. **Cut the least important element, not the clarity.** When a feature clutters the UI, remove its least
    important element; do not blur what the feature is for.
11. **Users choose what; Kove decides how.** Widgets, presets and their order are the user's; transitions,
    gestures, sizes and defaults are Kove's. Resist adding a setting when a good default will do.

| D-36 | CI environment: one job in a pinned Arch Linux container (`archlinux/archlinux:base-YYYYMMDD.0.N`) with pacman pointed at the Arch Linux Archive snapshot for the same date, so the base system and every package (Qt 6.12, Plasma 6.8, Node 26) are pinned together. Measured: the job takes about 2 minutes, so no package cache |
| D-37 | `main.qml` is the entry point and orchestrator (island lifecycle, popup and dialog, wiring); domain code lives elsewhere. Non-visual **services** in `contents/ui/services/` (`CommandRunner`, `AppLauncher`, `ControlLayout`, `MediaService`, `NotificationService`, `JobService`, `ConnectivityService`, `SystemControls`, `PanelController`): inputs through `required property` and injected collaborators, outputs through properties and signals, never reaching back into `root`. Pure decision **logic** in `contents/ui/logic/` (no QML or Plasma imports, Node-tested; the translated text is the one exception and lives in `Strings.qml`, which turns the logic's "which line applies" into the user's language). The island's own UI in `contents/ui/shell/`, reaching the coordinator as `app`, checked by `tests/app-api.test.cjs`. No QML singletons. **Command execution goes through `CommandRunner`** (one per island) and no service starts its own shell command; a read-only data engine, such as `AppLauncher`'s `apps` lookup, may live inside the service that owns it. Every new or moved file is born clean: `pragma ComponentBehavior: Bound`, required properties, zero warnings, and the per-file qmllint baseline and probe (D-36) go down with it. Plasma's global `i18n()` cannot be qualified, so its calls are confined to `Strings.qml` under one commented `qmllint disable unqualified` block | Two islands on two monitors must not share state, which rules out singletons. A single command runner keeps the engine's identical-command dedupe in one place and makes every shell call visible and mockable; read-only lookups carry no such risk. Services shaped this way are what the future widget catalogue (D-24, D-25) can wrap: Media, Notifications and Jobs become widget backends. Measured over Q2: `main.qml` 3,260 -> about 830 lines, 94 -> 190 Node tests, 42 -> 108 Qt Quick tests |
| D-38 | Notification priority is mapped from KDE's urgency enum explicitly (Low 1, Normal 2, Critical 4; anything else reads as Normal), never with a `>=` comparison. Order, highest first: Critical notification, keyboard layout announcement, Normal notification, job, media, idle. A Low notification is not an activity: it shows no popup, never replaces the one being presented, and only lands in the Control Center list. D-13 (the previous activity returns when the current one ends) and D-35 are unchanged | The old `>= 2` rule gave Normal and Critical the same top tier, so an ordinary message out-ranked a keyboard layout announcement and a job, while a Low one still popped up. Changing the order moved the shown activity in 6 of the 32 combinations of keyboard, job, notice kind and media, all intended (pinned in `tests/activity-resolver.test.cjs`). The notice's accent colour still treats urgency >= 2 as red; that is visual only and was not part of this decision |
| D-39 | The media player is chosen by an explicit filter and ordered sort keys, not points: eligible (never a stopped player; a player that is not playing needs some metadata), a browser with no metadata last, playing before not playing, most recently active first (the one that started playing last; among players that are not playing, the one that stopped last, so a just-paused player stays up), a native player before a browser, richer metadata, then the model's order. `MediaService` records per player (its bus name) when it last started and stopped playing and passes the times in; a player first seen already playing starts at its discovery time. The candidates are all players from `Mpris2Model` (the active player appears twice there; the same container is skipped), not Plasma's multiplexed single row | Ranking by metadata richness let a silent browser tab beat Spotify, and the multiplexer hid the other players, so the island could not rank what it never saw. Recency follows what the user just did. Against the points score the picks agree on 56,833 of 60,000 random player sets with equal times; the rest are the two intended cases (native over a richer browser at the same status; the bare-browser penalty is now a demotion). Pinned in `tests/media-players.test.cjs` |
| D-40 | The widget never applies a theme. The Control Center's former Light/Dark tile is "Theme settings": a click opens Global Theme settings (`kcm_lookandfeel`) through `AppLauncher` and closes the Control Center; its subtitle shows the current colour scheme, read only, from Qt's colour scheme. The module id stays `appearanceMode` so saved layouts keep working; `DarkModeControl` is not used anywhere. `tests/theme-safety.test.cjs` fails if any QML or JS file can apply or write a theme (`plasma-apply-*`, `lookandfeeltool`, `kwriteconfig`, the look-and-feel keys, a dark-mode switch) | Plasma's dark-mode control applies the light or dark global theme from `kdeglobals [KDE]` (`DefaultLightLookAndFeel` / `DefaultDarkLookAndFeel`), falling back to stock Breeze when they are unset, which rewrote a customised setup (colour scheme, widget style, cursor, window decoration, GTK files) and was not reversible. Switching themes is the user's job in System Settings (where Plasma's own automatic switching also lives); the island links to it. Reading the colour scheme from Qt also fixed the tile claiming "Light" on a dark desktop |
| D-41 | Cursor glow on expanded surfaces: a soft neutral-white glow follows the pointer on the expanded media, notification, job and keyboard-layout popups and on the Control Center, never on the compact shell. Intensity alpha(d) = I·e^(−k(d/R)²) with **R = 200 px, I = 0.05, k = 11**; the position follows the pointer by time-based exponential smoothing (**τ = 23 ms**, settled within 0.3 px) and the glow fades in and out over **350 ms**; static per-pixel noise of about ±0.5/255 dithers the banding; it is clipped to the shell silhouette (Notch shoulders, Pill curve, Control Center card) with the same shape item as a mask. It is an overlay above the content with no input handling: a passive `HoverHandler` plus a passive `PointHandler` (which keeps it following during slider and tile drags, when hover events stop). Follow and fade are skipped only when Plasma animations are disabled (`longDuration <= 1`); the durations are otherwise fixed. Values are tokens in `Tokens.js`. Two settings toggles (on by default): expanded popups, Control Center. **D-34 carve-out:** the glow renders frames only while the pointer moves, the position is settling or it is fading; zero frames at rest (including a resting pointer inside); it is exempt from the shared 8 fps decorative clock. It replaces the whole-island brighten on clickable notices (D-31). Shader sources live in `shaders/`, compiled to committed `.qsb` files by `scripts/build-shaders.sh`; `shaders/qsb.sha256` records the sha256 of every source and of every compiled `.qsb`; CI requires both to match the committed files and the sources to compile, which catches a stale, edited or swapped `.qsb` (a byte-for-byte rebuild comparison was tried and rejected: the container's qsb 6.12.0 emits different bytes than a desktop's for the same source) | A hover cue that follows the pointer reads as native to the shell and costs nothing at rest. A pointer-follow animation cannot be driven by the 8 fps clock without visibly stepping. Out of scope: compact shell glow, album-art tint, click ripple, frosted glass **Amended (D-43): opening the reply shelf plays a bloom: the glow springs to radius +60% and intensity 6% (K = 180, D = 14), holds 200 ms, then dissolves outward (radius x1.6 more, intensity to 0, K = 40, critically damped); no glow while the shelf is open, and the normal glow returns when it closes. Frames only during the bloom; none with animations off.** |
| D-42 | While the Control Center is open, media changes (play, pause, track, player) do not pull the island into the media popup: the user stays in the Control Center until they close it by any of its normal closing paths. The decision is a pure `suppressed` input to `bumpDecision`; it applies to media only, so a notification, job or keyboard announcement still pre-empts the Control Center as before | Pressing play or skip in the Control Center's media card used to swap the Control Center for the media popup mid-task. | D-43 | Inline reply shelf in the expanded notification popup, only for a notification that offers a reply action (`HasReplyActionRole`), on a top-edge panel, not in the Control Center list. It opens on a 200 ms hover dwell, once per hover session (after it closes it stays closed until the pointer leaves and returns), and never takes keyboard focus on its own: a click on the field focuses it. Esc closes the shelf first, then the popup; an empty, unfocused pointer-leave closes it. There is no scroll gesture. The island grows downward with a back-out spring (about 550 ms) and the shelf fades in a beat later; the message above stays as is. The window is sized for the shelf before a replyable notice first shows and keeps that size until the popup is hidden, never resizing while visible: a resize of a visible window shows one vertically stretched frame on Wayland (KWin presents the old buffer scaled to the new size; measured at the shrink, 120/176 = 0.68). The cost: a ~38 px transparent strip under replyable notice popups while they are shown that swallows clicks (a click there closes the island), because QML cannot trim a Dialog's input region (no mask property); a native helper could (`QWindow::setMask`). A replyable notice arriving over an already visible popup still resizes it once. The popup lifetime holds, and the notification is never expired by the island, while the field has focus or text; Plasma's own popup timer expiring it does not remove the reply action (checked on 6.8.2). Send calls `Notifications.reply(..., Close)` by id, then dismisses and collapses; if the row or action is gone the text is kept with a short inline error. Animations off: no spring, no bloom. Logic in `ReplyShelf.js`, state in `services/ReplyShelf.qml`. |
| D-44 | Scrollable Control Center content fades into the surface over 28 px at an edge only when more content lies beyond it; the strength ramps with the distance left to scroll, so it is crisp at the ends. The notifications list folds the fade into its existing rounded-corner mask (one gradient mask, no extra render pass). The page grid has no mask, so it uses two gradient overlays in the surface colour (no layer, no blur). Logic in `EdgeFade.js`. |
| D-45 | Colour encodes attention, never decoration: the urgency dot and content (album art, app icons, weather) are the only colour; every other surface, text and glyph is neutral graphite. | D-51 | The deck and its page-control capsule (D1). Pages on one horizontal axis: [Live] -> [presets, at most 3] -> [Control Center, always last]. Navigation is by clicks on a page-control capsule (Esc or an outside click closes); no swipe, scroll paging or arrow keys in D1. The capsule is a separate small pill floating about 7 px below the island, centred on it and following its bottom edge as it morphs (a pill in both shell shapes, with the island's surface and stroke, no cursor glow, no shadow because the island has none). Left to right in deck order: a theme symbolic glyph (D-26 fallback chains) for Live when it is in the cycle, a dot per preset, and the Control Center glyph; the current item is full white and an active dot stretches to a short 12 x 5 pill, the others are a muted neutral that brightens over 300 ms on hover (D-45, lightness only, `HoverHandler`); each item is a 22 x 22 click target. It is hidden with one page, in Control Center edit mode and while a pop-in shows, and comes back after the pop-in. It fades in (with a 4 px downward settle) once the island has finished opening and fades out first on close; clicks on its items navigate, clicks on its body do nothing and are not outside clicks. It is a satellite of the deck, not a second island (principle 1 holds), and it shares the deck's window. Fallback if three presets feel restrictive: a three-section capsule whose middle item shows and cycles the current preset. Pages pad all four sides equally (12 px), with the media artwork flush with that padding and concentric with the island's corner, and never grow for navigation. The visible body is as wide as the page in both shapes: the notch's concave ears flare 8 px outside the body (its item and the panel allocation are two shoulders wider), and content is laid out in the body, so Notch and Pill share one layout. Entry: a capsule click opens Live when media or a job runs (and the top activity is not a notice or keyboard announcement, which keep their pop-in), else the last-used page (session only, per island); a double-click opens the Control Center; the D-12 idle hot zones are unchanged. Transitions are horizontal (moving right in the capsule slides content left, by 24 px) with the silhouette morph to the page size and a cross-fade (340 ms, `easingStandard`); with Plasma animations off the silhouette snaps, only a short fade stays and the capsule appears without its settle.
